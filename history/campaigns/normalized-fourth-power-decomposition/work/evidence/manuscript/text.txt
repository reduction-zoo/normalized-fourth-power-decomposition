Abstract
We study exact membership in the cone of sums of real symmetric fourth powers, restricted to rational
tensors whose entries with even index multiplicities are all one. We give a deterministic polynomialtime reduction from complete sign-correlation matrix membership. The construction replaces each
source coordinate by a pair and explicitly specifies every target moment. Normalization forces every
real decomposition atom to have equal coordinate magnitudes, which yields rational sign-mixture
certificates and a direct recovery map from every valid target certificate. The resulting normalized
membership problem is NP-complete. The claim concerns this restricted cone, rather than positive
semidefinite tensor membership or approximate decomposition.

1. Introduction
Exact fourth-power decomposition asks whether a symmetric tensor is a sum of fourth tensor powers
of real vectors. This question arises in convex tensor formulations of high-order partitioning. Ke and
Honorio [1] study such a cone with a normalization that fixes every entry having only even index
multiplicities to one. Their Section 4 and Appendix C discuss the remaining complexity question for
this normalized domain.
The normalization matters. Hillar and Lim [2, Theorem 11.2] already state hardness of unrestricted
Grammian tensors, equivalently sums of fourth powers of linear forms. That statement does not
supply hardness with the prescribed entries fixed. We establish the restricted result through a complete
moment construction: each source sign is represented by a product of two target signs. All additional
moments receive explicit values, so the reduction requires neither a completion oracle nor a supplied
decomposition.
Theorem 1 (normalized membership). Let the input be a fully symmetric rational tensor of order
four and variable dimension 𝑑, with 𝑇𝑖𝑖𝑖𝑖 = 𝑇𝑖𝑖𝑗𝑗 = 1 for all 𝑖, 𝑗. Deciding whether it is a sum of fourth
powers of real vectors is NP-complete. There is a deterministic Karp reduction from complete signcorrelation matrix membership in dimension 𝑛 to this problem in dimension 2𝑛, with output length
𝑂(𝑛4 (𝐿 + log(𝑛 + 1))) when each source entry has at most 𝐿 encoded bits. The rule includes polynomial-time recovery of a source certificate from every valid rational sign-mixture certificate of its output.
The NP-hardness premise is the known full Boolean correlation-polytope membership theorem, attributed to Pitowsky [3] explicitly for complete matrices by Krause et al. [4]. We use its affine equivalence
with complete sign correlations [5, Section 4.5.2]. The original Pitowsky reduction was not inspected in
this work; our new tensor construction and its proof are self-contained once that stated source theorem
is assumed.

Malformed source strings are outside the source language. The total forward map sends them to the
dimension-two normalized tensor whose sorted entries are (1, 2, 1, 0, 1). Its entry of index multiplicity
three and one is two, which exceeds the absolute moment bound of one for sign mixtures; Lemma

output. Behavior outside that premise is unspecified.

6. Relation to prior tensor hardness
Hillar and Lim [2, Section 11] identify Grammian tensors with positive combinations of fourth powers
and state their unrestricted hardness. Their displayed matrix construction sets 𝑆𝑖𝑗𝑘𝑙 = 𝐴𝑖𝑗 when 𝑖 = 𝑘
and 𝑗 = 𝑙, and zero otherwise. For the all-one two-dimensional matrix this yields 𝑆1212 = 1 and 𝑆1122 =
0. Thus the displayed implication does not supply full symmetry or the prescribed normalization. This
observation does not declare their theorem false or rule out a different proof. Our result supplies a direct
complete moment map under the stronger fixed-entry restriction.
Ke and Honorio’s normalized cone [1] matches the input domain here at order four. Their additional
balance constraint and their larger PSD relaxation are not imposed by our rule. The result consequently
resolves normalized cone membership without asserting hardness of their entire statistical optimization
program. Complete decomposability is another name for the unrestricted power-sum cone; the literature
audit also checked this formulation.

The implementation and finite checks accompany this manuscript in the campaign directory. Python
3.14 and Z3 5.1.0 were used for testing; the candidate itself uses only Python’s standard library. From
that directory, run:
python3 work/check.py --self-test

python3 reviews/paired-sign/check.py
typst compile work/manuscript.typ work/manuscript.pdf

The candidate reads a JSON matrix from standard input and returns the complete normalized tensor.
Its --extract mode reads the source plus a target certificate and returns a source certificate. work/
contract.md defines the rational strings, sorted tensor indices and mixture format. Neither mode calls
the testing oracles.
The prepared suite and separate verification program passed 41 instance comparisons and 26 recovery
calls. Source dimensions were at most three; the verification source oracle uses exact interval and
tetrahedron criteria. Target oracles enumerate sign vertices and solve rational linear feasibility, then
check returned moments with exact fractions. Additional independent checks covered a four-dimensional YES/NO pair, recovery from a 97-term split and sign-flipped certificate, and 715 coordinates of
a five-dimensional mask example. These experiments test actual subprocess outputs. They are finite
corroboration, not substitutes for the general proof.
